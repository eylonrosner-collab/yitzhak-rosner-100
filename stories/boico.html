סתיו 1945 · בוקרשט

Meeting Boico

מאת יצחק (ז׳אק) רוזנר

Hi Andre

I don’t know if you ever heard the story of the first meeting between me and my cousin Bibi Rosner/Mihai Boico . Anyhow here it is:

Somewhere in the autumn of 1945, I was living in Bucharest and started to study at the Politechnical School. It was a hard time for everybody but especially hard for me and my family. To undertain myself and help my parents, we just returned after 3 years in Transnistria, I started to work for a company called Slova which distributed newspapers from Bucharest to the whole country. I did my job between 3:00 and 8:00 in the morning (at Gara de Nord) and at 8.15 went to school. At noon I had to report to the office present the daily report and get instructions for next day.

One day, when I got to the office all the people there were excited. I was told that earlier in the day an officer, probably a Lieutenant- Colonel was looking for me. He was told to come back later, at the hour when I was supposed to be at the office.

This was a big surprise for me. You have to understand that as a Jewish youngster I never had any connection with Rumanian officers except when we were transported to Transnistria. I could not understand what can be the cause for a Rumanian officer to try to meet me.

After half an hour the officer entered our office and asked me 2 times what my name was, then he asked me to come out with him because he wants to speak with me. Imagine how I trembled of fear. We went out on Sarindar street, along the Cercul Militar, and when we got on Calea Victoriei he asked me if I am the son of Littman Rosner . After I confirmed he asked me “Did you ever hear about a person called Bibi Rosner? Now as a child I many times heard in family talks about this my cousin. It was some kind of legend. We knew that he was in France than in the Spanish civil war but afterwards nothing. Nobody knew what happened to him. There was no connection whatsoever between the family and Bibi all the years of the war. To my total surprise he told me: It is me, do look to my nose, this is a Rosner nose. I was totally shocked.

Then we sat down at Capsa, a well known Bucharest conditory, ordered coffee and I had to tell him the story of all the family during and after the war. He asked for their addresses and had special interest for Mendel Wasserman (Meir’s father). Than he gave me a short description of his life during these years. After the defeit in Spain he got to Moscow and worked in the soviet automotive industry (at Ziss). He married and had a and He also told me how he got to me. When he came to Bucharest (he was then in the Divizia Horia Closca si Crisan) he immediately started to look for his family, a thing which was not easy in this post war years. One day by total chance he met on the street an old boyfriend from Moldovitza, Leo Podolier- who somehow knew my whereabouts and told him how to find me – the only family member living at that time in Bucharest.

There were no cell phones and no phones at all at that time so I wrote immediately to my parents and to the Wassermanns and ….the rest is history.
